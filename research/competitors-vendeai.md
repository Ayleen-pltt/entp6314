# Competitor Comparison: Sales, Inventory, and Profit Apps for Small Sellers

**Companion to:** [opportunity-scan-vendeai.md](opportunity-scan-vendeai.md) and [complaints-vendeai.md](complaints-vendeai.md).
**Compiled:** 2026-09-29.
**Scope (my reading of the prompt's placeholder):** the top 5 products that help small and informal sellers record sales, track stock, and see profit from a phone. I picked them because they recur in VendeAI's target markets and in the review data: **Treinta, Kyte, Loyverse, Square POS, Sortly**. Meta's new Seller app is listed as a sixth one to watch, without a full profile.

**Method.**
- **Pricing** comes from each company's own pricing page or press release.
- **Features and target customer** come from each company's homepage.
- **Funding** comes from press coverage. It is only shown where the round was publicly announced.
- **Complaints** come from Apple App Store reviews rated 1–3★, dated 2025-03-29 to 2026-09-29, pulled verbatim from Apple's public review feed (details in [complaints-vendeai.md](complaints-vendeai.md)).
- Anything I inferred rather than read on a source is marked **[UNVERIFIED]**.

## What does nobody serve well?

1. **Recording sales that stays free and stays free.**
   - Treinta removed its free tier in 2026, and that change produced most of its complaints.
   - Kyte's free tier leaves out inventory management, which starts at the $13.99 Grow plan ([pricing](https://www.kyteapp.com/pricing)).
   - Sortly's free plan stops at 100 items.
   - Loyverse and Square are free, but they are built for a counter or card reader, not for someone selling over chat. **[UNVERIFIED: this is my inference from their feature pages. I did not test how well they handle chat orders.]**
   - The market has a free-to-paid trap, and users are angry about it.

2. **Stock and profit numbers a seller can trust.**
   - Kyte is the only one here built around WhatsApp and Instagram orders, and 1 in 4 of its negative reviews say stock or profit numbers are wrong.
   - None of the five advertise a way to check that recorded stock matches reality, such as quick recounts or flagging suspicious changes. **[UNVERIFIED: based on their public feature pages only.]**

3. **How informal selling actually works.**
   - The top feature requests are: sizes and colors, sales on credit ("fiado") and installments, separate wholesale and retail prices, and working offline.
   - Treinta and Kyte reviewers keep asking for these, which suggests they are missing or weak. **[UNVERIFIED: inferred from the complaints, not from testing the apps.]**

4. **Recording a sale by chat or voice instead of forms.**
   - Every product above records a sale through POS screens.
   - The closest thing is Kyte's "automatic product registration via AI" and its "Smart Assistant", but only in the $19.99 Prime tier ([pricing](https://www.kyteapp.com/pricing)).
   - I found no product that turns "sold 3 black dresses at $40, she pays half Friday" into a sale, a stock change, and an amount still owed. **[UNVERIFIED: I did not test Kyte's AI features, and new products may exist that I didn't find.]**

5. **Pricing advice based on the seller's own costs.**
   - Meta suggests prices based on comparable listings. Kyte shows profit per sale.
   - None of the five advertise a price recommendation built from the seller's cost, fees, and target margin, which is the proposal's "minimum / target / premium price". **[UNVERIFIED: based on public pages only.]**

**What this means for VendeAI:** the opening is **not** "an AI assistant." Kyte already sells one, and Meta gives pricing help away for free. The opening is being **the free, chat-first sales record that gets the numbers right and handles credit, variants, and wholesale pricing.** The paid tier would then be pricing and marketing help built on that data. The risk is that Kyte fixes its reliability problems, or Meta adds profit and stock tracking to Seller, before VendeAI reaches its first users. **[UNVERIFIED: this is a forecast.]**
